島国  ·  An Island Nation

Japan is measured
in seasons,
not just miles.

From the volcanic spine of Hokkaido to the coral shallows of Okinawa, the archipelago stretches nearly 3,000 kilometers — a single country that holds snow country and subtropics at once.

14,125 islands, by the government's 2023 count
125M people, on land smaller than California
1,600+ years since the first imperial court at Nara
季

01The Four Seasons

Shiki — 四季 — is not a season calendar so much as a national aesthetic. Food, clothing, poetry and even train announcements shift with it.

春

Spring

Sakura season moves north across the country like a slow tide, usually starting in Kyushu in late March and reaching Hokkaido by May.

夏

Summer

Humid and cicada-loud. Fireworks festivals (hanabi taikai) and cotton yukata robes mark the months from July to early September.

秋

Autumn

Momiji — maple viewing — draws crowds to temple gardens in Kyoto and Nikko as leaves turn from late October into November.

冬

Winter

The Sea of Japan coast buries towns in some of the heaviest snowfall on Earth, while Tokyo often stays dry and clear.

国

02Eight Regions

Japan is conventionally divided into eight regions, each with its own dialect, cuisine, and temperament.

  • HokkaidoNorthern frontier — dairy, seafood, deep snow
  • TohokuRural north — rice, hot springs, folklore
  • KantoTokyo and the eastern plain
  • ChubuAlps, Mount Fuji, and Nagoya's industry
  • KansaiKyoto, Osaka, Nara — the old heartland
  • ChugokuHiroshima, the Inland Sea coast
  • ShikokuSmallest main island, pilgrimage routes
  • Kyushu & OkinawaSubtropical south, Ryukyu heritage
食

03On the Table

Washoku

Traditional Japanese cuisine was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013, prized for balancing five colors, five tastes, and five cooking methods.

Regional bowls

Ramen alone splits by region — miso in Sapporo, tonkotsu in Hakata, shoyu in Tokyo — each broth tied to local history and climate.

Tea & ceremony

Chanoyu, the tea ceremony, treats a single bowl of matcha as an exercise in hospitality, patience, and quiet attention.

心

04Everyday Craft

Mono no aware

物の哀れ — a gentle awareness of impermanence — runs through everything from falling cherry blossoms to the deliberate wear of old wood.

Omotenashi

おもてなし describes hospitality anticipated before it's asked for — the reason a ryokan inn feels considered down to the last detail.

Ma

間 — the deliberate pause or negative space — shapes architecture, music, and conversation alike, valuing what's left unsaid.
